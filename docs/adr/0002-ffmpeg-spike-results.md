# ADR-0002: FFmpeg WASM spike results

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-08

## Context

[ADR-0001](0001-client-side-audio-extraction.md) committed to in-browser audio
extraction with single-thread ffmpeg.wasm, but two load-bearing claims were
unverified: (1) a ~1 GB, 1-hour 720p video can be processed without hitting the
2 GB WASM memory ceiling, and (2) wall-clock time is acceptable on the
audio-only path (no published benchmark measures this exact case). The
playbook's pivot trigger was: repeated OOM on ~1 GB inputs even with WORKERFS,
or wall-clock beyond ~20 minutes → switch to mediabunny/WebCodecs.

## Decision

**The spike passes decisively — ffmpeg.wasm stays; no pivot to mediabunny.**

Measured setup: `@ffmpeg/ffmpeg` 0.12.15 + single-thread `@ffmpeg/core` 0.12.10
(self-hosted, ESM build), WORKERFS input mount, fresh FFmpeg instance per job,
args `-vn -sn -dn -map 0:a:0 -ac 1 -ar 16000 -c:a libopus -b:a 16k output.ogg`.
Environment: MacBook Air M5 (24 GB), macOS 27.0, Google Chrome 150.0.7871.47
(headless, driven by `scripts/drive-spike.mjs`), Nuxt 4 **production build**
(`pnpm build` + node preview server).

### Measurements (2026-07-08)

| input | MB in | duration | mount | wall-clock | MB out | realized kbps | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fixture-30s-720p.mp4 | 0.9 | 0:30 | workerfs | 144 ms | 0.07 | 18.8 | done |
| fixture-30s-720p.mp4 | 0.9 | 0:30 | workerfs | 140 ms | 0.07 | 18.8 | done |
| fixture-30s-720p.mp4 | 0.9 | 0:30 | workerfs | 141 ms | 0.07 | 18.8 | done |
| fixture-30s-720p.mp4 | 0.9 | 0:30 | memfs (debug) | 134 ms | 0.07 | 18.8 | done |
| fixture-30s-720p.mp4 | 0.9 | 0:30 | workerfs + classWorkerURL escape hatch | 145 ms | 0.07 | 18.8 | done |
| **fixture-1h-720p.mp4** | **1045.8** | **1:00:00** | **workerfs** | **16.0 s** | **8.11** | **18.9** | **done** |
| **fixture-1h-720p.mp4** | **1045.8** | **1:00:00** | **workerfs** | **16.5 s** | **8.11** | **18.9** | **done** |
| **fixture-1h-720p.mp4** | **1045.8** | **1:00:00** | **workerfs** | **16.0 s** | **8.11** | **18.9** | **done** |

Key facts established:

- **1 GB / 1-hour 720p → 8.11 MB Opus in ~16 s** (~225× real-time), three
  consecutive runs in one page with no reload and no OOM. WORKERFS keeps the
  input out of the WASM heap; the fresh-instance-per-job policy (terminate
  after `readFile`) held memory flat across runs. Renderer JS heap peaked at
  ~31 MB (note: `performance.memory` is main-thread only; the WASM heap lives
  in the worker — the no-crash 3/3 result is the meaningful signal).
- Output verified with ffprobe: ogg/opus, mono, full 3600.0 s duration,
  18.9 kbps realized → **~8.5 MB/hour, 3× under Groq's 25 MB cap** (headroom
  to ~3-hour meetings without chunking).
- `libopus` + ogg muxer are present in the default single-thread core (also
  confirmed from the upstream build script), as is `libmp3lame` (plan B, unused).
- The Vite/Nuxt **production build handles ffmpeg's nested worker correctly**
  with `vite.worker.format: 'es'` + `optimizeDeps.exclude` — the
  `classWorkerURL` escape hatch (self-hosted `/ffmpeg/esm/worker.js`) was
  verified working but is not needed.
- Progress events flowed correctly for the `-vn` run; the worker additionally
  derives progress from stderr `time=` lines as a fallback (documented
  bogus-value bug in ffmpeg.wasm issue #600 did not manifest).
- `createDir('/work')` before `mount(WORKERFS, ...)` is required — `mount()`
  does not create the mount point.

## Consequences

- The product's core promise (client-side extraction, <25 MB uploads, no video
  leaving the browser) is now measured fact, not inference. Single-thread stays;
  core-mt and mediabunny remain rejected/stretch respectively.
- Extraction time is negligible next to upload/transcription; no UX pressure to
  add a faster path.
- The spike page (`/spike/ffmpeg`) and driver (`scripts/drive-spike.mjs`)
  remain in-tree for regression measurement on future browser/core upgrades.

## Caveats / follow-ups

- The 1-hour fixture is synthetic (`testsrc2` video + 440 Hz sine AAC audio,
  generated by `scripts/make-fixture.sh` at higher bitrate). A sine decodes and
  encodes faster than real speech, so treat 16 s as the size-class datapoint;
  **re-run once with a real ~1-hour meeting recording** and append the row here.
  The order of magnitude (seconds, not minutes) will not change.
- Safari was not tested (nested workers are the known risk there); Chromium is
  the demo target. Firefox untested. Test both before Phase 11 polish.
- Wall-clock includes core load (~100 ms from HTTP cache). First-ever visit
  pays a one-time ~31 MB core download.
